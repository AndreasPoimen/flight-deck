Aerospace Atlas — How it works and how to keep it useful

Prepared 15 October 2026. This guide describes the local version in this folder. The company directory is a researched snapshot, not a live global business registry.

1. What is available now

The atlas contains 299 company or business-unit entries and 488 mapped locations across 41 countries. This update adds 40 employers and 104 locations to the previous 259-company snapshot. ClearSpace has five sites, and Airbus has 77, including Stevenage, Portsmouth, Filton and Broughton. Subsidiaries may also have their own entry, so this is not a count of independent corporate groups.

The app has an interactive globe, a detailed street map, sector colours, country and region filters, a startup/scaleup filter, a searchable company directory, estimated-size sorting, company details, careers/contact links, and a company-density overlay. The company list remains accessible without operating the canvas. Select a company to highlight its sites; expand the locations list in its details to choose another site. Search for a place such as Stevenage to find the relevant site directly.

Drag or use arrow keys to rotate the globe. Use the mouse wheel, pinch, +/- controls or +/- keys to zoom. Globe magnification ranges from 0.7× to 50×. Detailed map has street-level zoom up to level 19. Extra map detail does not improve the precision of the company coordinates. Use the official address link before planning a visit.

2. Where the information comes from

Company descriptions, sectors and startup categories are editorial summaries. Records are maintained in dist/data.js; the browser does not search the web for employers. Official websites, contact pages, recruitment pages and company location publications are the preferred evidence. Registry and industry-directory pages help discover candidates and establish identity.

Each record has a company source URL. Each location has its own source URL, a publication-confirmation flag and coordinate-precision label. The companion company-sources.csv lists every mapped site and its evidence, so individual claims can be audited. For an unconfirmed site, its URL is a research lead, not proof of that location.

No names, emails or addresses have been collected from private accounts. Contact buttons open public company routes; the atlas does not send messages, subscribe users, or apply for jobs.

3. How trustworthy is the snapshot?

235 of the 488 locations have a published location source recorded; 253 remain unconfirmed. 176 company records have a recorded website review. These are different checks: a company can have a reviewed homepage while its location remains unconfirmed. Original records retain their earlier review date; additions from this update are dated 2026-10-15. A review date describes our research, not a guarantee that the source is current.

The “Published locations only” checkbox excludes locations without recorded supporting publication. A published location can still be a registered office or recruitment location rather than an engineering campus. Relevant records say so. Even published addresses can move or become outdated. All current coordinate precision labels are city-level; some approximate a campus, but the dataset does not claim survey-grade coordinates.

Size bands in older entries are explicitly unverified estimates. New entries use Unknown rather than inventing headcounts. Unknowns sort after known bands in either size-sort direction. Startup/scaleup is an editorial discovery category: an acquired business or mature venture may still sit in that category pending review. There is no automated incorporation, insolvency or ownership check running today.

Coverage favours the UK, Europe and North America and is uneven elsewhere. Engineering suppliers, satellite operators, launch providers and space service firms are included. Not every aerospace company or every site is present. Website availability is not proof that an employer is currently recruiting. For example, a careers page can outlive a business: Gravitilab's current homepage directs enquiries to insolvency administrators, so it was not added from its older recruitment page (https://gravitilab.space/).

4. What the density colours mean

Turn on Company density to colour fixed 2-degree latitude by 2-degree longitude cells. Each cell counts distinct directory company IDs matching the current search, region, country, type, sector and publication filter. Several sites of one company in the same cell count once. The same company can count in different cells. Separately listed subsidiaries count separately.

The fixed colours mean 1, 2–4, 5–9 and 10+ companies per cell. They do not rescale when filters change. This makes filtered comparisons more understandable. The overlay appears in both map views; ordinary company pins remain selectable. Pins at the same point open a chooser rather than concealing other companies.

This is a binned company-count heat map, not a measurement of employment, vacancies, investment or contracts. Two-degree cells do not have equal areas: their east-west width shrinks with latitude. A blank cell means no matching records in this directory, not no aerospace activity. Company selection can highlight additional sites outside the current filters; those highlights are not added to the filtered totals or density calculations.

For a future analytical heat map, use an equal-area grid or aggregate by administrative region; provide separate layers for unique employers, verified operating sites, dated vacancies and contract awards. Do not multiply a company's global headcount by every office or assign a nationwide job count to its headquarters. Those would produce misleading hotspots.

5. Map technology and the lag fix

The globe is a Canvas 2D orthographic projection using D3 7.9.0. It looks three-dimensional because longitude/latitude points are projected onto the visible hemisphere; it is not a terrain or WebGL simulation. TopoJSON Client 3.1.0 converts world-atlas country boundaries derived from Natural Earth. Geography is fetched from jsDelivr at runtime.

The globe loads 1:110-million geography first and then 1:50-million detail. While dragging, zooming or auto-rotating, it uses the lighter geometry. After interaction stops, detailed boundaries are restored. If detailed loading fails, coarse geography remains available. If neither loads, the UI reports the failure and provides Retry. Dependencies and map data require an internet connection.

Movement requests are combined into one paint per browser animation frame. A separate canvas caches the geographic background when the view is unchanged, so filtering and density toggles do not recompute all coastlines. High-DPI rendering is capped at 1.5×. The globe does not continuously repaint when idle, and auto-rotation stops when the page is hidden. The street map uses Leaflet 1.9.4 and canvas-rendered company markers. Local map layers are refreshed only while that view is active.

During local browser checking, one detailed globe paint measured about 172 ms and one moving, lower-detail paint about 27 ms. These are indicative samples on this machine, not a formal frames-per-second guarantee. The main improvement is less work per moving frame and no unnecessary idle redraw. Deep zoom and lower-powered devices can still benefit from Detailed map.

Detailed map requests OpenStreetMap street tiles. Attribution remains visible. No bulk downloading or offline tile harvesting is implemented. The light/dark toggle changes the atlas interface and globe; the standard street-map tiles retain their provider styling.

Mapping references:
- D3 geographic projections: https://d3js.org/d3-geo/orthographic
- World Atlas source: https://github.com/topojson/world-atlas
- Natural Earth: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/
- Leaflet: https://leafletjs.com/
- OpenStreetMap attribution: https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright

6. What updates live today

Only configured public job boards are fetched live. Rocket Lab, SpaceX, Planet Labs and Spire Global currently have Greenhouse board tokens. On company selection or Refresh, the browser calls:

https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{board_token}/jobs

It displays the returned total, retrieval time and up to five postings, with the careers link for the rest. These are company-wide postings across all locations, not jobs at the selected pin. A 12-second timeout and request cancellation prevent stale responses from overwriting another company's panel. Failed refreshes can show the last successful response from the current browser session, clearly labelled. If no response is available, the careers page is the fallback.

Greenhouse's public GET job-board endpoints do not require authentication. This app never submits applications. Other companies currently have careers/contact links rather than integrated feeds. No feed means “not connected”, not “not hiring”. Source: https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html

The company list, addresses, size bands and location evidence are not automatically refreshed. Opening the page does not discover newly incorporated companies. There is no scheduler, backend database or live company-update service installed in this version.

7. A practical route to ongoing company discovery

Recommended next step: a daily UK discovery job plus a weekly international review queue. This would be scheduled updating, not a claim to capture every new company worldwide in real time.

A. Companies House (UK)

The official advanced-search API supports incorporation dates, status and SIC filters. It requires an API key. Aerospace manufacturing (30300), aircraft/spacecraft repair (33160) and satellite telecommunications (61300) are useful starting categories, supplemented by engineering and software terms. Broader terms improve recall but require more manual relevance checks. UK records will miss overseas companies and aerospace startups registered under general software or consultancy codes.

The company information stream provides change events with a timepoint cursor. It could detect changes promptly, but needs a continuously connected service, saved cursor and reconnect/backfill logic. A daily scheduled query is the simpler first implementation. The documented default REST limit is 600 requests per five minutes; use conservative pacing and handle 429 responses. Keep the API key in server-side secrets, never in the browser bundle.

References:
- Search API: https://developer-specs.company-information.service.gov.uk/companies-house-public-data-api/reference/search/advanced-company-search
- Change stream: https://developer-specs.company-information.service.gov.uk/streaming-api/reference/company-information/stream
- Guidelines: https://developer.company-information.service.gov.uk/developer-guidelines
- SIC categories: https://resources.companieshouse.gov.uk/sic/

B. Industry and incubator discovery

UKspace membership, ESA's business network and BIC ecosystem, and the Satellite Applications Catapult catalogue provide relevant leads that company-registration categories miss. These are discovery sources, not automatically reusable global feeds. A published directory is not evidence of a documented bulk API or permission to copy its database. Check available exports, reuse terms and permission before automating collection; otherwise review new entries manually and confirm facts on the company's own site.

- UKspace: https://www.ukspace.org/membership/
- ESA network: https://business.esa.int/business-network
- ESA incubation overview: https://business.esa.int/esa-space-applications
- Catapult catalogue: https://sa.catapult.org.uk/space-capabilities-catalogue/preview/
- Global supplier leads: https://satsearch.co/suppliers

C. Official company updates

Monitor published newsroom RSS/Atom feeds where available, permitted careers APIs, and official office/contact pages. Detect changes and queue them for review. A new job location is a clue, not automatic proof of a permanent facility. Check broken links and domains periodically because ownership and business status can change. Do not scrape private social profiles or infer personal contacts.

8. Proposed update architecture — not installed yet

Sources → scheduled collectors → raw snapshots → candidate/review queue → approved company and site records → versioned public snapshot → map.

Give each company a stable ID and, where available, registry number and jurisdiction. Keep aliases, parent/subsidiary relationships and website changes separately. Deduplicate by registry identity first, then domain plus reviewed name; never merge solely because two organisations share an address.

Keep companies, sites, source observations and vacancies as separate tables. Every observation needs source URL, retrieved timestamp, source publication date when available, evidence type and review status. Give sites stable IDs, an address type (operating / registered / recruitment / unknown), precision (address / campus / city / region), coordinates and verification date. The current runtime site ID is company ID plus array position; production maintenance should replace this with stable stored site IDs before frequent automated updates.

Collectors should save a cursor, follow pagination, use a small overlap window, deduplicate by source ID, respect limits, retry transient failures with backoff and keep the last good snapshot on errors. Persist a failed-run status instead of replacing the map with an empty response. Suggested cadence: daily new-company candidates, weekly company-site link checks, monthly status review, and job refreshes on demand or with a short server cache. These are recommended cadences, not existing schedules.

Candidates should require a relevance decision and an official operating-location check before becoming trusted map records. Record closures, acquisitions and renames instead of silently deleting history. A registered company may be dormant, and a registered-office postcode may belong to an accountant. Do not geocode such an address as an engineering plant.

For hosting, use a small server-side collector with persistent storage and a scheduler, then serve cached public JSON to browsers. This avoids exposing keys or making every visitor query upstream sources. A long-lived streaming consumer would require infrastructure suited to persistent connections. Before activation, choose the provider, obtain any necessary keys, confirm reuse rights and set an operating budget. None of those services or credentials has been created here.

9. Public aerospace and defence contracts: feasible

Yes. A separate layer of public procurement opportunities and awards can be built and linked to company records. This update researches feasibility only: no contracts are imported, mapped or presented as live opportunities.

UK: Find a Tender offers OCDS JSON releases, with date/stage and process identifiers. It is the main source for new procurements following the February 2025 transition; Contracts Finder remains useful for applicable older records. MOD's Defence Sourcing Portal is another official route, and MOD guidance describes publication to the government portals. Start with Find a Tender, retaining procurement IDs to deduplicate later notices.

- UK API overview: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/open-contracting
- Find a Tender: https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/
- Contracts Finder API: https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/apidocumentation
- MOD sourcing guidance: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mod-contracting-purchasing-and-finance-e-procurement-system/contracting-purchasing-and-finance-cpf-tool

EU: TED's public Search API supports published notices, bulk XML and CPV / place-of-performance geography. The documented search operation is POST /v3/notices/search and does not require authentication. Geography may be a NUTS region, so a shaded region can be more honest than a precise pin. Reference: https://docs.ted.europa.eu/api/latest/search.html

US: USAspending offers award searches and separate recipient versus place-of-performance filters. SAM.gov's Opportunities API exposes notices and available place-of-performance fields, requires an API key and documents daily updates to active notices. Awards and opportunities are different datasets and should remain distinct layers.

- USAspending API: https://api.usaspending.gov/docs/intro-tutorial/
- Data interpretation: https://www.usaspending.gov/federal-spending-guide
- SAM.gov API: https://open.gsa.gov/api/get-opportunities-public-api/

Location must be explicit: use place of performance when published; otherwise show a labelled supplier address, buyer address, or a region-level location. Never silently replace missing work locations with the supplier's headquarters. Multi-site and nationwide awards should not be assigned to one precise factory. Records without usable geography should remain searchable in a list, with “location not published”.

Expected fields vary by source and notice: title, buyer, supplier when awarded, publication/deadline dates, status, amount/currency when disclosed, category codes, source URL and location evidence. An award ceiling, estimated tender value and obligated spend are different measures; amendments and multi-lot notices must not inflate totals. Public disclosure is incomplete, especially for sensitive work, so this would not represent every defence contract.

Feasibility was checked against official documentation. Some individual Find a Tender notice pages returned access errors in this research environment; no end-to-end contract ingestion or field-completeness test was performed. A sensible next phase is a small UK pilot to inspect actual notice fields together before building the global layer.

10. Files and maintenance

- dist/index.html — main page, controls and dialogs.
- dist/styles.css — responsive layout and dark/light interface.
- dist/app.js — filters, map projection, density cells, selection, live jobs and map interactions.
- dist/data.js — the company snapshot; edit this to maintain records.
- dist/guide.html — readable, printable version of this guide, linked from the map.
- dist/guide.txt — plain-text guide for Notepad.
- dist/company-sources.csv — one row per site, including source and precision.
- scripts/validate.mjs — syntax, schema, region coverage, combined-filter and density-deduplication checks.
- research/ — research working files and backups; not part of the served website.
- .openai/hosting.json — retained Sites identity and static dist configuration.

To run validation from the project folder: node scripts/validate.mjs

Open “Open Aerospace Atlas.html” or dist/index.html in a browser. A static HTTP server is preferable for consistent browser behaviour. External scripts, geography, fonts, tiles and job feeds require connectivity; the directory uses bundled data and the font stack has a system fallback. There is no account database, application form submission or browser geolocation permission. Only the theme is stored in localStorage; fetched job results are cached in memory for that tab session.

Maintain records conservatively: use a unique company ID, list each known city once per company, preserve company/source dates, keep unknown sizes null, and validate before sharing a new snapshot. A location source must actually support the site before setting verified=true. Add new countries to the region mapping. Review mergers, dissolved businesses and repurposed domains. Export the source CSV again after data changes so it stays aligned.

Local checks cover data consistency and selected browser flows; they do not certify every company, address, job or external link. Keep the publication status separate from the local files: the existence of a Sites project ID does not mean a public website has been deployed.


11. DATA REUSE, PRIVACY AND CORRECTIONS

This is an independent research directory, not a complete global register. Each company and location is an editorial record with source links. A public source link or published fact is not, by itself, permission to reproduce a third-party database, text, image or directory. This snapshot has not had a source-by-source rights audit for every record, so this guide does not certify every item for every kind of reuse. The app uses original short descriptions and text initials; it does not intentionally reproduce company logos, photographs or long passages from company websites. It does not call the OpenCorporates or Dun & Bradstreet APIs, import SAM.gov entity data, or currently publish a contracts dataset. Check API terms and public-display rights before using any future automated feed.

The directory uses public company contact routes and may show general company inboxes. It is not intended to publish directors' details or personal contact information. It has no accounts, application forms, analytics or advertising trackers. The selected theme is saved in this browser's local storage. To load maps and connected job feeds, a visitor's browser requests resources from GitHub Pages, jsDelivr, OpenStreetMap's tile service and, for four employers, Greenhouse's public job-board API. Those services may receive ordinary request information such as the visitor's IP address; each provider's own terms and privacy practices apply.

For a correction, outdated entry or removal request, use https://github.com/AndreasPoimen/flight-deck/issues. The issue tracker is public, so do not include private or sensitive personal information. Atlas content is reviewed manually; no automated legal, ownership or freshness check is running.
